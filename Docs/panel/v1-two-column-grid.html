Ten toggles, ten LEDs, three jacks. The width you pick barely matters — the vertical budget is what decides this board. A 3U panel gives you about 110 mm of usable height between the rails, and a single column of ten toggles plus three jacks needs roughly 160 mm. So the channels go into two columns no matter what, and the HP you choose only buys you finger room.

The layout that doesn't work

A single column of channels — the layout that reads most naturally, one row per pulse, top to bottom — cannot be made to fit, and it's worth seeing the arithmetic before you try:

10 toggles × 13 mm + 3 jacks × 15 mm = 175 mm needed vs. ~110 mm available

Even with sub-miniature toggles at a cramped 10 mm pitch and jacks packed to 12.5 mm, you're still at 137 mm. There is no single-column version of this module.

Reading the drawings

Channels run in reading order — 1 and 2 on the top row, 7 and 8 on the fourth. Each toggle is on-off-on: up routes that pulse to Bus A, down to Bus B, centre is off. The LED sits outboard of its toggle and taps the signal before the switch, so it always shows that channel firing whether or not it's routed anywhere.

Channel 8 is the normalled one. It runs from PULSE8 off the ribbon — the bit the stock Pulses never breaks out — until you patch the EXT jack, at which point the jack's break contact hands that channel over to whatever you plugged in.

The two mode toggles sit below the channel block, one per bus, each selecting OR or AND for that bus.
